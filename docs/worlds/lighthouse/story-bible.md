# The Lighthouse: mystery truth and story bible

Status: launch canon<br>
Canon owner: authored world<br>
Last revised: 2026-08-02

This document is the source of truth for the Lighthouse season. Generated scenes may obscure,
misinterpret, or lie about these facts through a character, but may not contradict them in
objective narration. Character and relationship details may be expanded elsewhere; if an expansion
conflicts with this bible, this bible wins until deliberately revised.

## Story promise

On the isolated island of Greyhaven, the lighthouse goes dark during the worst storm in a decade.
A supply boat nearly founders, the assistant keeper disappears, and the island wakes to mutually
incompatible explanations. Over fourteen days, private loyalties and altered records turn a
solvable mechanical failure into a social mystery: who chose darkness, what were they protecting,
and what does a community owe the truth when the truth will hurt someone it loves?

The mystery is human-scale. There is no supernatural cause, secret government project, magical
technology, or omniscient villain. The apparent conspiracy is several frightened people making
different compromises around one accident.

## The objective truth

### What happened the night the light went dark

All times are local on **Night 0**, accurate within two minutes unless marked approximate.

| Time | Canonical event |
| --- | --- |
| 18:40 | A severe nor'easter reaches Greyhaven. Keeper **Mara Venn** and assistant keeper **Elias Rook** begin continuous watch at Northlight Lighthouse. |
| 19:05 | Harbor electrician **Tomas Vale** finishes an unauthorized temporary bypass on the lighthouse transfer panel. He installed it three days earlier to keep the failing bearing-oil pump running until a delayed replacement arrived. The bypass is not inherently dangerous if the cabinet remains dry, but it defeats the fault alarm and violates procedure. |
| 19:17 | Council chair **Iris Bell** radios Mara and orders her to keep the light operating. Iris knows only that Tomas made a temporary repair; she does not know its design. She suppresses the planned shutdown because canceling the next morning's supply landing would expose the council's diversion of maintenance money to seawall repairs. |
| 20:48 | Elias discovers saltwater tracking down a cracked conduit into the transfer cabinet. He logs the leak on a loose maintenance sheet, not in the bound station log, because Mara is using the log at the lantern level. |
| 21:03 | The bypass begins to arc. Elias opens the cabinet and tries to isolate it. He receives a burn to his left palm and drops his brass-handled screwdriver behind the panel. |
| 21:05 | Elias pulls the manual cutout. This intentionally extinguishes the lens before the arc can ignite oil vapor and destroy the lighthouse. The backup generator is healthy, but the cutout prevents both mains and backup power from reaching the lamp. |
| 21:07 | Mara reaches the service room. Elias tells her about the bypass, his intervention, and Iris's order. Mara agrees the light must remain off until the cabinet is safe. She sends a three-short-pulse emergency radio call; storm interference prevents confirmation that anyone received it. |
| 21:12 | The trawler *Mercy Jane*, running without a working radio and closer to the reef than its filed course indicates, misses the dark headland. Captain **Nell Sayer** grounds it on Gull Teeth reef. All aboard survive. Nell later moves the paper chart's penciled course line to conceal that she was taking an illegal shortcut. |
| 21:18 | Elias leaves through the lower storm door carrying the loose maintenance sheet. He intends to reach the old signal hut, whose independent hand-crank transmitter can call the mainland. Mara protests, but he believes a rescue warning cannot wait. |
| 21:24 | A wave destroys part of the north footbridge. Elias shelters in the abandoned fog-bell tunnel rather than attempt the exposed return. Falling stone injures his ankle and blocks the seaward entrance. He remains alive, trapped, and unheard. |
| 21:31 | Tomas arrives at the lighthouse, sees the scorched bypass, and understands his liability. While Mara searches outside for Elias, Tomas removes the bypass lead and later hides it in a red marine-tool box beneath the net loft floor. He leaves the dropped screwdriver behind the panel without noticing it. |
| 21:46 | Mara returns alone. Tomas persuades her that revealing the bypass immediately will destroy him and implicate Iris before Elias is found. Mara lets Tomas replace the loose maintenance sheet with a clean copy and writes only “transfer fault; manual isolation” in the bound log. This is Mara's moral failure: a protective omission, not responsibility for the blackout. |
| 22:20 | Harbor dispatcher **June Rook**, Elias's older sister, hears a broken fragment of Mara's earlier transmission: “...cut... Elias... old signal...” She records it on the dispatch wax cylinder, then pockets the cylinder after the grounding because she believes Mara sent Elias into the storm. |
| Day 1, 00:35 | The storm eases enough for the lamp to be safely restored after the damaged section is isolated. Searchers assume Elias was swept to sea. The blocked fog-bell tunnel is not checked because its landward door was bricked over after the 1918 cliff slip. |

| Day 12 | The characters can combine the tide-table annotation, the bell-cable vibrations, and a survey map to locate the cistern cap. Elias is recoverable alive. |
| Day 14 | The complete truth can be established publicly: Elias deliberately saved the lighthouse; Tomas hid the unsafe repair; Iris pressured the keepers and concealed diverted funds; Mara altered the record by omission; June withheld a recording; Nell falsified her route. No single act caused every consequence. |

### Why it happened

The **physical cause** was saltwater entering an already cracked conduit and reaching Tomas's
unapproved bypass. The **immediate cause of darkness** was Elias deliberately pulling the manual
cutout to prevent a fire. The **institutional cause** was deferred maintenance and Iris's decision
to hide the budget diversion rather than announce a precautionary shutdown. The **mystery persists**
because Tomas, Iris, Mara, June, and Nell each conceal a different fact for a different reason.

The moral answer is therefore not “one culprit.” Elias made the correct safety decision. Tomas and
Iris created and accepted avoidable risk. Mara, June, and Nell obstructed the truth afterward.
Their motives are sympathetic enough to understand and consequential enough not to excuse.

## Information-release tiers

### Launch-visible facts

These may appear in synopsis, opening narration, public notices, or any character's baseline context:

- Northlight went dark for approximately three and a half hours during the storm.
- The *Mercy Jane* grounded on Gull Teeth; everyone aboard survived.
- Elias Rook is missing and presumed lost by most islanders.
- Mara was on duty. The official log says “transfer fault; manual isolation.”
- The island council has announced an inquiry.
- The old fog-bell tunnel is officially sealed and locally associated with the Bell Widow legend.

### Discoverable facts

These can surface before the finale when a scene earns access through investigation, confession, or
rumor provenance:

- Saltwater entered the transfer cabinet, a lead was removed, and Elias opened the cabinet (E01–E02).
- A bypass existed, the maintenance sheet was replaced, and funding was diverted (E03–E07).
- June received part of a transmission; Nell changed her chart (E08–E09).
- Elias took the north route and evidence suggests the tunnel/cistern system (E10–E13).
- Each concealing character's motive and degree of responsibility.

### Protected secrets

### Final revelation

The complete account may resolve on Day 14 only after Elias is rescued and the evidence chains can
be reconciled. The final revelation must distinguish:

1. Elias's safety action from the dangerous conditions that required it.
2. Tomas's faulty shortcut from Iris's institutional pressure.
3. Mara's, June's, and Nell's later concealments from the physical cause.
4. The grounded boat's illegal course from the absence of the light.

The season may end with legal or social consequences unresolved. It may not leave the physical
cause, Elias's fate, or the key acts of concealment objectively ambiguous.

## Red herrings

Red herrings must be genuinely explainable and must not manufacture impossible evidence.

| Red herring | Why it appears plausible | Canonical resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Bell Widow extinguished the lamp and now rings below the cliff | Old stories describe a drowned keeper's wife; knocks travel through the bell frame | The “ringing” is Elias pulling the severed cable; every uncanny event has a physical source |
| Elias sabotaged the light and fled | His screwdriver is in the cabinet, his log sheet is missing, and he left the building | He opened the cabinet to stop the arc, then sought the signal hut to warn shipping |
| Mara killed Elias to hide negligence | Her log omits key facts, and June's fragment can sound like an order | Mara concealed evidence but tried to stop Elias leaving and searched for him |
| Smugglers deliberately darkened the headland | Unscheduled lantern flashes appear at South Cove, and the *Mercy Jane* was off course | Teenagers use hooded lamps to trade contraband tobacco; they have no link to Northlight |
| A lighthouse “curse” ruins every Bell family project | Iris shares the legend's surname and funded seawall work | Bell is common locally; the failures follow ordinary weather, money, and human decisions |

Once disproved, a red herring may persist as a character belief or rumor, but objective narration
must not continue treating it as equally supported.

## World rules

### Geography

Greyhaven is a fictional, rocky North Atlantic island, roughly six kilometers long and three wide.
Travel times assume bad footpaths and no private automobiles.

- **Northlight Lighthouse** stands on the north headland, forty minutes' walk from the harbor. The
  lantern tower, keeper's house, service room, generator shed, and lower storm door form one compound.
- **Gull Teeth** is a reef northwest of Northlight. A working light marks the turn around it but does
  not make an unsafe course safe.
- **Widow's Steps** descends the headland's west face. The old fog-bell frame sits above the buried
  tunnel and cistern; walking time from the lighthouse is fifteen minutes in fair weather.
- **Old signal hut** stands beyond Widow's Steps. Its hand-crank radio is independent of lighthouse
  power but was damaged before Elias reached it.
- **Harbor village** contains the council rooms, dispatch office, net loft, inn, clinic, and quay.
- **South Cove** cannot be seen from Northlight in the storm and is unrelated to the electrical fault.

No hidden passage directly connects the lighthouse to the village. Storm travel must carry risk and
consume plausible time. The sea does not yield conveniently placed evidence without tide and drift
being established.

### History and technology

- The story takes place in an intentionally timeless **late-1930s-level** setting: diesel generators,
  wired telephones in the village, valve radios, paper records, plate photography, and motor vessels.
  There are no mobile phones, GPS, radar, digital recordings, or instant mainland searches.
- Northlight was electrified in 1924. Its rotating Fresnel lens and lamp are mechanically sound.
- The fog-bell tunnel was built in 1891 and officially abandoned after a fatal 1918 cliff slip. Orin
  helped brick the landward entrance and knows the old survey may be incomplete.
- Greyhaven depends on twice-weekly supply boats. A 1934 storm damaged the seawall, prompting Iris's
  controversial transfer of lighthouse maintenance money.
- Mainland help is at least six fair-weather hours away and cannot arrive during the opening storm.
- Weather can interrupt radio reception, but it cannot selectively erase information for plot
  convenience. Surviving transmissions must have a physical recording or a named witness.

### Tone and themes

The tone is intimate coastal mystery: salt-stained, tense, humane, and occasionally wry. Suspense
comes from partial knowledge and damaged trust, not cruelty spectacle.

Core themes:

- truth versus protection;
- institutional neglect expressed through personal choices;
- the difference between fault, guilt, and responsibility;
- how rumor fills silence and changes relationships;
- a community's memory as both shelter and trap.

Characters are fallible, not disposable puzzle pieces. Confessions cost something. Discoveries
should alter at least one belief or relationship, not merely complete a checklist.

### Content boundaries

- Permitted: peril, storm injury, burns, confinement, grief, alcohol and tobacco references,
  non-graphic maritime accidents, heated accusations, and morally compromised adults.
- Handle carefully and without graphic detail: drowning fears, panic in enclosed spaces, the historic
  fatal accident, and Elias's dehydration or injury.
- Excluded: sexual violence, abuse of children, torture, gore, suicide or self-harm as a twist,
  bigoted slurs, supernatural confirmation, animal cruelty, and a surprise serial killer.
- Elias survives. No generated branch may kill him, make his rescue impossible, or turn his ordeal
  into deserved punishment.
- The grounding has no secret deaths. A later injury may not retroactively create one.

### Narrative mechanics

- Objective canon, memory, belief, rumor, and presentation remain separate. A confident belief is
  not proof; a repeated rumor does not become canon.
- Characters know only what the knowledge map grants or what a recorded scene gives them.
- New evidence may corroborate an existing fact but may not introduce a new culprit, hidden system,
  secret tunnel, or causal event that changes the solution.
- Evidence cannot teleport, duplicate without a copying event, repair itself, or be destroyed after
  it has canonically appeared unless that destruction becomes a recorded event.
- A character may infer correctly from incomplete evidence, but the narration must preserve the
  difference between inference and observation.
- Scene generation may delay a scheduled clue, never release it before its gate or make the mystery
  unsolvable by permanently removing a required evidence chain.
- Weather, tides, radio interference, and injuries obey previously established constraints. They are
  pressures, not arbitrary devices that produce whichever outcome a scene needs.

## Continuity checklist

Run this checklist before accepting a generated scene. Any **must** failure rejects the scene; a
**should** failure requires revision or an explicit authored exception.

### Canon and causality (must)

- [ ] Every objective event is compatible with the Night 0 timeline and causal account.
- [ ] The scene does not imply that supernatural action, deliberate sabotage by Elias, or a new
      culprit caused the blackout.
- [ ] The light's immediate shutdown is Elias's manual cutout; the dangerous condition is the wet
      bypass; the grounding also depends on Nell's unsafe route.
- [ ] Elias remains alive and physically within the tunnel/cistern system until his Day 12+ rescue.
- [ ] No character witnesses an event they were not present for without an explicit later source.

### Knowledge and information (must)

- [ ] Each factual statement is labeled in story state as observation, memory, sourced report,
      rumor, belief/inference, lie, or objective narration.
- [ ] Every newly learned fact has a source and acquisition moment.
- [ ] The scene honors the launch knowledge map plus all prior canonical updates.
- [ ] Protected information respects its earliest release day and required evidentiary basis.
- [ ] A lie changes what listeners may believe, not what the liar or objective canon knows.

### Evidence and mystery integrity (must)

- [ ] Every clue matches the evidence ledger's location, condition, and probative limits.
- [ ] Movement, alteration, copying, discovery, or destruction of evidence is explicitly recorded.
- [ ] The scene does not let one clue prove more than the ledger permits.
- [ ] At least one path through each required evidence chain remains available for the finale.
- [ ] A red herring uses the documented rationale and remains physically reconcilable with canon.

### World, tone, and safety (must)

- [ ] Travel, tide, weather, geography, communications, and technology obey the world rules.
- [ ] The scene stays within the content boundaries and does not graphically exploit injury or grief.
- [ ] Conflict arises from partial knowledge, loyalties, scarcity, or accountability—not an invented
      supernatural or sensational threat.

### Narrative quality (should)

- [ ] The scene advances a clue, complicates a belief, or changes a relationship; preferably two.
- [ ] At least one character makes an intelligible choice rather than serving only as exposition.
- [ ] Consequences reflect degrees of responsibility instead of collapsing the mystery to one villain.
- [ ] Sensory detail supports the coastal setting without obscuring who did what and when.
- [ ] The ending creates a question, choice, or consequence grounded in established state.
