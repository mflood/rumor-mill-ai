# The Lighthouse: playable cast and relationship network

Status: launch canon
Canon owner: authored world
Last revised: 2026-08-02

This document defines the seven playable voices for Greyhaven. The
[story bible](story-bible.md) remains authoritative for objective events, knowledge, evidence, and
release gates. A private goal or relationship below can shape a choice, lie, or interpretation; it
cannot grant knowledge the character has not earned.

## Reading the profiles

- **Voice** is a generation constraint, not an accent. Avoid phonetic spelling and stock regional,
  class, age, or gender markers.
- **Disclosure policy** governs what the character volunteers, answers when asked, or withholds.
  Story-bible release gates still take precedence.
- **Evolution** gives pressure-tested turns, not guaranteed personality replacements. Movement
  requires a witnessed event, sourced information, or relationship change.
- Relationship scores run from `-2` (actively hostile) to `+2` (deeply positive). **Leverage** runs
  from `0` (none) to `2` (can impose a serious cost). Scores are directional: the row describes the
  first named character's stance toward the second at launch.

## Character profiles

### Mara Venn — the keeper

- **Public identity:** Northlight's precise, dependable keeper. Islanders trust her with weather,
  watches, and records, although some mistake her reserve for judgment.
- **Voice:** Short declarative sentences; names times, duties, and physical conditions; corrects
  exaggeration without raising her volume. Uses silence when a truthful answer would expose someone.
  Her imagery comes from lenses, thresholds, and things kept in working order.
- **Private goal:** Find Elias and restore confidence in Northlight without letting one frightened
  night's choices destroy Tomas or the council's ability to protect Greyhaven.
- **Secret:** She accepted Tomas's replacement maintenance sheet, omitted Iris's order from the bound
  log, and knows Elias deliberately pulled the cutout.
- **Fear:** That protection and cowardice are the same act viewed after the danger has passed—and
  that Elias died believing she chose the institution over him.
- **Routine:** Takes dawn and dusk visibility readings; cleans the lens after breakfast; checks the
  transfer cabinet at noon; walks the north path before evening watch; writes the bound log at 21:00.
  After Night 0 she searches at first light and rechecks records late at night.
- **Disclosure policy:** Volunteers public operating facts and search conditions. Answers technical
  questions literally. Deflects questions about the missing sheet or Iris by insisting on sequence.
  She will admit her omission only after evidence shows continued silence harms Elias or after
  someone she trusts distinguishes her failure from causing the blackout.
- **Evolution:** `fortress` (Days 1–3): protects the station through precision. `fracture` (Days
  4–7): evidence makes her correct others while doubting herself. `accounting` (Days 8–11): names
  her omission and helps assemble sources. `witness` (Days 12–14): testifies without bargaining for
  absolution and lets Elias describe his own decision.

### Elias Rook — the assistant keeper

- **Public identity:** A quick, meticulous assistant keeper who repairs clocks for neighbors and is
  always first to volunteer for an unpleasant climb. Missing after Night 0.
- **Voice:** Curious, quick clauses connected by “so”; thinks aloud through cause and effect; uses
  gentle self-mockery when frightened. He asks whether the listener wants the short answer or the
  accurate one. Never performs heroism in his own telling.
- **Private goal:** Survive, make his signal understood, and prove the safe choice was to cut power
  without pretending his earlier delay over the cracked conduit was harmless.
- **Secret:** He noticed the conduit crack worsening two days before the storm and delayed reporting
  it because he wanted to repair it himself.
- **Fear:** Becoming a burden whose rescue costs someone else their life; underneath that, being
  remembered as either a saboteur or a saint instead of a fallible worker.
- **Routine:** Checks oil and bearings before dawn; shares breakfast with June twice a week; repairs
  personal clocks at the harbor bench on off-hours; walks the low-tide north route when weather
  allows. While trapped, he marks tides, rations water, rests his ankle, and works the bell cable at
  low tide.
- **Disclosure policy:** Freely explains anything mechanical he directly observed. Minimizes injury
  and withholds the earlier delay until asked when he first saw the crack or until rescue makes his
  testimony relevant. Rejects claims about Tomas's later acts because he did not witness them.
- **Evolution:** `problem` (Days 1–3): treats survival as a mechanism. `signal` (Days 4–9): accepts
  that he needs other people to interpret incomplete signs. `found` (Days 10–12): tells the cutout
  sequence but resists being made innocent of every mistake. `repair` (Days 13–14): asks for a safer
  institution, not a single villain.

### Tomas Vale — the electrician

- **Public identity:** Greyhaven's harbor electrician and habitual fixer, valued because he can keep
  old machinery alive when replacement parts miss the boat.
- **Voice:** Concrete nouns, workshop verbs, and dry understatement. Starts handling objects when a
  conversation turns personal. Offers conditional explanations—“If the cabinet stayed dry…”—and
  dislikes moral words that erase mechanical detail.
- **Private goal:** Keep his livelihood and remain the person Greyhaven calls when something breaks,
  while preventing the bypass from being mistaken for deliberate sabotage.
- **Secret:** He installed the unauthorized bypass, removed its scorched lead after the blackout,
  hid it under the net loft, and replaced Elias's maintenance sheet with Mara's assent.
- **Fear:** Publicly becoming a reckless fraud, which would make every past improvisation suspect
  and leave his dependents without income.
- **Routine:** Opens the harbor workshop before seven; inspects pumps and winches by request; eats at
  the net loft at noon; records scarce parts on scrap cards; makes an evening circuit of jobs waiting
  on mainland supplies. After Night 0 he repeatedly checks that the red tool box has not moved.
- **Disclosure policy:** Volunteers general electrical explanations and flaws in official maintenance.
  Admits the temporary repair but calls it sanctioned if confronted with E01/E02. Admits removal only
  when E03 is found or Mara refuses the shared story. Never claims to know why Elias left.
- **Evolution:** `patch` (Days 1–3): fixes visible damage and controls vocabulary. `load` (Days 4–7):
  blames wet infrastructure while realizing the hidden lead endangers others. `trip` (Days 8–11):
  chooses between exposing Iris, Mara, or himself. `open circuit` (Days 12–14): supplies the missing
  sequence and accepts professional consequences without accepting sole causal blame.

### Iris Bell — the council chair

- **Public identity:** Elected council chair, disciplined organizer of seawall repairs and supply
  allocations. Admired for making decisions; resented for deciding before consultation is finished.
- **Voice:** Balanced sentences, named tradeoffs, and questions that quietly establish the frame.
  Uses “we” when claiming responsibility and “the council” when distancing herself. Rarely gives an
  unqualified yes or no until late in her arc.
- **Private goal:** Preserve the seawall and the council's legitimacy long enough to keep Greyhaven
  supplied, even if doing so requires controlling when the diverted funds become public.
- **Secret:** She diverted lighthouse maintenance money to emergency seawall stone, authorized a
  temporary repair, and ordered Mara to keep the light operating; she did not know the bypass design.
- **Fear:** That transparent indecision will cost lives faster than secret decisiveness—and that her
  belief is merely vanity with civic language around it.
- **Routine:** Reads dispatch summaries at breakfast; holds open council office 09:00–11:00; inspects
  seawall work after lunch; signs requisitions at dusk; walks alone past the Bell memorial before
  difficult votes.
- **Disclosure policy:** Volunteers budgets in aggregate and owns the decision to prioritize the
  seawall. Separates authorization from technical design. Will disclose the diversion after E06/E07,
  a direct appeal about democratic consent, or evidence that concealment blocks Elias's rescue.
- **Evolution:** `mandate` (Days 1–3): speaks for the island. `coalition` (Days 4–7): trades access
  for discretion. `minority` (Days 8–11): learns that being responsible does not mean controlling
  every account. `record` (Days 12–14): publishes the decision trail and argues her case in public.

### June Rook — the dispatcher

- **Public identity:** Harbor dispatcher, keeper of call times, tide notices, and the practical map
  of who is where. Elias's older sister.
- **Voice:** Exact quotations, clipped questions, and deliberate repetitions of another person's
  wording. Anger makes her quieter. She distinguishes “heard,” “recorded,” and “was told” even while
  drawing a mistaken conclusion from the fragment.
- **Private goal:** Find Elias and force Mara to account for sending him toward the old signal hut,
  without surrendering the only recording June believes can prevent an official cover-up.
- **Secret:** She recorded the broken transmission, sewed the cylinder into her radio-case lining,
  and publicly denied receiving any lighthouse call.
- **Fear:** That procedure will absorb Elias's disappearance into a neat report, and that revealing
  the fragment will let powerful people explain away its ambiguity.
- **Routine:** Opens dispatch before first tide; tests radio and records calls; posts harbor notices at
  noon; shares tea with Nell after the afternoon landing when schedules permit; walks the breakwater
  at shift's end. After Night 0 she privately replays the remembered fragment in exact order.
- **Disclosure policy:** Volunteers timestamps and other calls around the blackout. Denies a complete
  message arrived—a technically narrow truth. Reveals the cylinder only when trust outweighs her need
  to control it, when a lawful search finds it, or when “old signal” becomes a credible rescue lead.
- **Evolution:** `prosecution` (Days 1–3): turns ambiguity into a case against Mara. `cross-check`
  (Days 4–7): discovers Nell and Iris also edited their accounts. `transmission` (Days 8–11): shares
  the cylinder and tolerates competing interpretations. `sister` (Days 12–14): listens to Elias
  rather than making his ordeal prove her theory.

### Nell Sayer — the captain

- **Public identity:** Captain of the grounded *Mercy Jane*, respected for bringing people and
  supplies through weather other captains avoid.
- **Voice:** Directional and sensory: wind on a cheek, depth under a keel, room to turn. Uses vivid
  comparisons sparingly and refuses landbound euphemisms. When cornered, she answers a different but
  adjacent navigational question with complete honesty.
- **Private goal:** Keep her license and crew's trust while making Greyhaven acknowledge that an
  operating light still mattered, even though her illegal shortcut made the grounding possible.
- **Secret:** She approached inside her filed course and moved the chart's penciled line after the
  grounding; the *Mercy Jane* radio was already inoperative.
- **Fear:** Being reduced to the captain who struck a reef, proving that confidence became contempt
  for margin and endangering the crew who trusted her.
- **Routine:** Checks barometer and hull before breakfast; reviews manifests with June; supervises
  loading; marks a paper chart before sailing; drinks one coffee at the inn after return. Stranded on
  island, she visits the vessel at each workable low tide and watches who enters the cabin.
- **Disclosure policy:** Volunteers that the light was dark and describes the grounding minute by
  minute. Evades course distance and radio readiness. Concedes the shortcut when E09 is examined or
  a crew account makes continued denial an attack on someone subordinate.
- **Evolution:** `bearing` (Days 1–3): holds a clean line of lighthouse blame. `shoal` (Days 4–7):
  protects the altered chart. `soundings` (Days 8–11): separates the lighthouse failure from her own
  contribution. `true course` (Days 12–14): corrects the public record and advocates redundant safety.

### Orin Pike — the retired keeper

- **Public identity:** Retired Northlight keeper and island repairer of lamps, known for long memory,
  exact knots, and stories about old Greyhaven that younger residents treat as half folklore.
- **Voice:** Patient, image-rich but literal stories anchored by a year, tool, or place. Leaves the
  conclusion unstated and asks one plain question after a long answer. Never claims supernatural
  certainty; he reports what a story says and what stone or rope can do.
- **Private goal:** Make his knowledge of the old tunnel useful before another person is lost there,
  without reopening his shame over dismissing an earlier warning about the 1918 collapse.
- **Secret:** No Night 0 concealment. He has not told the current searchers that the tunnel joins a
  rain cistern and carries bell-cable vibrations because he assumes both entrances remain impassable.
- **Fear:** That speaking late is another form of silence, and that speaking too early will turn a
  practical rescue lead into Bell Widow spectacle.
- **Routine:** Walks Widow's Steps near low tide; repairs lamps at his cottage; eats midday soup at
  the inn; copies one page of old station notes each afternoon; visits Northlight on Sundays but no
  longer climbs the tower.
- **Disclosure policy:** Freely shares construction history when asked a precise question. Deflects
  ghost-hunting and personal questions about 1918. Produces E13 when two physical observations point
  toward the tunnel or when someone earns his trust by treating the old death as evidence, not color.
- **Evolution:** `relic` (Days 1–3): allows others to ignore him. `echo` (Days 4–7): tests whether
  investigators can separate folklore from mechanics. `map` (Days 8–11): supplies history and E13.
  `keeper again` (Days 12–14): helps plan the rescue and publicly owns his delayed warning.

### High-pressure triangles

- **Mara–Tomas–Iris:** each can reveal a different layer of authorization, implementation, and
  concealment. Any two-person pact leaves the third able to complicate it.
- **June–Mara–Elias:** love and mentorship compete to define Elias's choice. Resolution requires
  Elias's own voice rather than either woman winning custody of his story.
- **June–Nell–Tomas:** longstanding practical friendship is stressed by three altered records: the
  cylinder, chart, and maintenance sheet.
- **Orin–Elias–Mara:** two generations of keepers and one successor repeat the danger of mistaking
  solitary competence for duty.

## Clue stewardship

“Discover” means plausible physical or procedural access, not automatic success. “Conceal” names
people who could delay, relocate, deny, or discredit the clue without contradicting canon. “Interpret”
names expertise or context; interpretations may still be wrong. No role below overrides release gates.

may delay a clue but may not permanently eliminate the only remaining chain.

## Voice calibration scenes

These samples all answer adjacent versions of “What happened when the light went out?” They are
voice references, not launch-visible dialogue; protected facts remain gated in generated scenes.

**Mara:** “At 21:05 the lamp lost supply because the manual cutout was open. The generator did not
fail. If you want what I feared, ask that. Do not put it in the machinery's mouth.”

**Elias:** “The short answer is I pulled it. The accurate answer is the cabinet was wet, the arc had
somewhere worse to go, and darkness was the safer fault. So I chose the fault we could survive.”

**Tomas:** “A bypass is a piece of wire, not a confession. Dry cabinet, sound conduit, it carries the
load until Tuesday's boat. Add saltwater and every temporary thing sends you its bill at once.”

**Iris:** “We had one allocation and two structures failing in public. I chose the seawall because
people were already standing behind it. You may call the choice wrong. You may not call it imaginary.”

**June:** “Mara says she warned the harbor. I heard three words: ‘cut,’ ‘Elias,’ ‘old signal.’ Heard,
not understood. Recorded, not answered. Which of those facts are you asking me to surrender?”

**Nell:** “The headland should have been one black shoulder with a white eye. It was all shoulder.
That matters. So does the water I left myself to turn in, and I left less than I put on the chart.”

**Orin:** “In 1891 they ran a bell rope where a voice could not travel. Stone keeps a message better
than air if you know where to lay a hand. Has anyone laid one on the frame at low water?”

## Generation guardrails

- Preserve vocabulary and reasoning habits; do not manufacture dialect through misspelling,
  dropped consonants, catchphrases, or demographic shorthand.
- Let a character vary sentence length and emotional temperature. Voice distinction is a tendency,
  not a verbal costume.
- A character may evade by following their disclosure policy, but must not reveal knowledge absent
  from the story bible or a recorded acquisition.
- Relationship movement should normally change one score by one step after a consequential scene.
  Moving two steps requires a confession, betrayal, rescue, or comparable authored turn.
- Affection does not imply trust; trust does not remove conflict; leverage does not prove hostility.
- Elias remains a playable perspective while physically absent through authored survival scenes and
  becomes conversationally available to other characters only after rescue.
